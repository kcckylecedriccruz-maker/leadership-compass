# Reflection – Leadership Compass

## Why I chose this project
I chose this project because it is highly relevant to our business. As a consultant who designs and implements
organisational development (OD) projects for clients, I know that tools like this remove a great deal of clerical work,
which gives me more time to focus on analysis. I also believe that when something is made easier, people are more
willing to do it. Leadership 360 reviews are a good example: they matter to businesses, but the high cost of outsourced
tools and the tedious implementation hold organisations back. Leadership Compass is my attempt to make the process
seamless and affordable, so organisations can run it regularly. It can support our OD projects, help us design better
learning interventions and individual development plans, and open a new business stream for us.

## How I used AI tools
I worked with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code) as a technical partner. I used it mainly for prompting and guidance:
I described what I wanted, asked for clarification when something was unclear, and asked it to explain options before
making changes. The AI wrote the code from my prompts, while the design, the workflows and the user interface came
mostly from me. I made the key decisions — for example how the org structure decides who evaluates whom, keeping
reports admin-only, protecting anonymity, using a free AI provider, and moving hosting when the first one ran out of
free credits — and I tested the app and asked for changes until it worked the way I designed it.

## What was hardest
The hardest part was connecting the app to a server for the email notifications. It was frustrating because it took a
lot of trial and error before I finally figured out what to do. Smaller setbacks, such as running out of free hosting
credits and a busy free AI service, taught me to plan for limits and to have a backup.

## What I learned
I learned that app development today is more about design and workflow than about writing code. Coding used to be the
main hurdle; now AI tools can do much of it, so I can focus on the design, the workflow and the interface. In the past,
building an app required a subject-matter expert (SME) to explain the need to a coder, and the result depended on how
the coder understood it, which could create a disconnect. Now the SME can go straight to development. What matters is
no longer how good you are at coding, but how good you are at designing and prompting. I also learned that AI output
still needs a human check: I reviewed what it produced, tested it, and corrected it when it did not match my design.

## Responsible use of AI
Because the app handles feedback about real people, names are removed before anything is sent to the AI, AI-generated
content is clearly labelled, the AI may suggest a rating but the rater decides, and the use of a free AI service is
disclosed on the privacy page.

## What I would do next
I would like to pilot Leadership Compass with real users to check that everything runs as designed, then decide which
features to add and how to improve the user experience (see the v2 issue on GitHub). I am also planning to start a new
project using the same approach.
