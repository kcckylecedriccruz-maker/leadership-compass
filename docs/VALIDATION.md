# Idea validation – Leadership Compass

**Owners:** Kyle Cedric Cruz and Vivian Cruz

Seven validation steps, with the evidence behind each. Sources are linked; evidence from our own client work is
described without naming clients or providers.

## Step 1 – Problem statement
Leadership 360 reviews are highly relevant to organisations, but the biggest challenge is **implementation**: running a
review is tedious, and the results are hard to consolidate and analyse. Because of this, many organisations do not run
360 reviews regularly. Those that do often pay outsourced providers a lot for access to assessment tools.

## Step 2 – Target user
- **Primary:** HR, organisational development (OD) and learning teams who run leadership reviews, both inside
  **Formidea** and at **Formidea's client organisations** (Formidea is a business consultancy and learning provider).
- **Secondary:** employees who give and receive feedback, and managers who coach their teams.
- Context: Philippine workplaces where English and Filipino are both used.

## Step 3 – Current alternatives and their gaps
| Alternative | Gap |
| --- | --- |
| Outsourced providers with paid 360 tools | Expensive; organisations pay a lot for access, so reviews are run rarely or only for a few leaders |
| Not running 360 reviews at all | The most common outcome because implementation is too much work; leaders miss feedback that is relevant to their development |
| Manual approach (forms, files, spreadsheets) | Tedious to set up and chase; consolidating and analysing results by hand takes a lot of time |

## Step 4 – User evidence
- **Client feedback from past engagements.** Clients we have worked with on organisational development have expressed
  the same views: 360 reviews are relevant and valuable, but implementation is tedious and results are hard to
  consolidate and analyse, so they are rarely done. Clients also noted that outsourced tools are expensive.
- **Our own experience at Formidea.** In our OD projects we have seen that the workload of running a review and the
  cost of outsourcing the tool are the main reasons reviews are not done regularly.
- **External evidence of the pain.** A 2025 survey of 1,000 US workers found **79%** would opt out of 360 feedback if
  they could, often citing bias ([LiveCareer](https://livecareer.com/resources/careers/360-degree-feedback);
  [SHRM](https://shrm.org/topics-tools/news/organizational-employee-development/employees-reject-360-degree-reviews-cite-bias)).
  This is a résumé-service survey and is treated as weaker evidence, but it supports our focus on clear items and
  strong anonymity.
- **Limitation:** the client evidence comes from conversations during past engagements, not from formal interviews.
  Next step: confirm with 3–5 clients in a short structured interview or survey during the pilot.

## Step 5 – Market evidence
- **Businesses in the Philippines:** about **1.25 million** business enterprises operated in 2023 (PSA List of
  Establishments, via [DTI MSME Statistics](https://dti.gov.ph/businesses/msmes/msmeresources/msme-statistics)).
  The same source reports that over 99% are MSMEs. The PSA's 2023 data also counts about **110,000 small** and
  **4,800 medium** establishments nationally — the size range most likely to run structured leadership reviews
  without an in-house tool ([PSA regional release, 2025](https://rssocar.psa.gov.ph/system/files/attachment-dir/CAR-SSR-2025-26_MSMEs_in_the_Cordillera_2023_ULE.pdf)).
- **Use of 360 feedback:** a 2013 benchmark of 211 companies found **47%** used 360 results in performance management
  and **70%** used them for career development ([EBSCO summary](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/business-and-management/360-degree-feedback));
  about **85% of Fortune 500** companies were reported to use 360 feedback in 2009
  ([summary](https://star360feedback.com/what-percentage-of-corporations-use-360-surveys-in-their-leadership-development-processes)).
  We did not find a recent independent figure; these show that 360 feedback is mainstream among larger organisations
  and much less used among smaller ones.
- **Leadership training spend:** global estimates range from about **US$27 billion**
  ([Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/global-corporate-leadership-training-market)) to higher figures
  depending on scope ([Training Industry](https://trainingindustry.com/research/leadership/the-state-of-the-leadership-training-market/)).
  Assessments like 360 reviews feed directly into that spend through development plans and learning interventions.

## Step 6 – Differentiating angle
- **Seamless implementation:** evaluations are generated from the org structure (self, manager, skip-level, upward,
  peers, external raters), with bulk upload, secure links for people without accounts, automatic reminders and
  review-status tracking — removing the tedious setup that stops organisations today.
- **Automatic consolidation and analysis:** reports by rater group with anonymity rules, readiness and gaps, plus AI
  summaries, organisation-wide insights and themes from written feedback.
- **From feedback to development:** AI-suggested interventions per focus area, 30/60/90-day plans and manager feedback
  — supporting better **learning interventions and individual development plans (IDPs)**.
- **Local and affordable:** English and Filipino help for raters; runs on free tiers, so it is far cheaper than
  outsourced tools.
- **For Formidea:** supports our OD projects and opens a **new business stream** (offering the tool and analysis to
  clients).

## Step 7 – Riskiest assumption and test
- **Assumption:** if implementation is seamless and affordable, organisations will run 360 reviews regularly.
- **Test:** pilot one review cycle with Formidea and 1–2 client organisations. Measure setup time, completion rate
  before the cycle closes, and whether the client plans a second cycle within 6–12 months.
- **Success signal:** setup in under a day, at least 80% completion, and at least one client committing to a repeat
  cycle or paid engagement.
- **Result:** to be recorded after the pilot.
